# P1.5 — the stable-by-construction build: the escape regime is *intrinsically* unstable

_Cope Labs / Seth · 2026-06-20 · model [tip_model_p15_stable.py](tip_model_p15_stable.py) ·
figure [p15_escape.png](p15_escape.png) · the re-audit's identified next step._

## The build

The re-audit said: build a stable model so the escape test can actually run. I did the
principled reduction — adiabatically eliminate **both** fast layers: the free virus (Vw,Vt
clear ~23×/day ≫ cell turnover 1×/day) **and** the immune effectors (QSS). That leaves a
**4-variable cell-only competition** (T, Iw, It, Id) with no fast lag. WT R0 = 8.70 preserved.

## Result — my hypothesis was wrong, and that's the finding

I expected the oscillation was the free-virus lag. **It isn't.** With the virus eliminated,
the ν<1 cases oscillate as badly or worse than P1.4 (tail max/min 694× and 11037×, vs the
stable 1.000 at ν=1). The instability is **intrinsic to the cell-level TIP↔WT competition
once the TIP's production cells (Id) escape killing (ν<1)** — an under-damped predator–prey
loop, not a lag that a quasi-steady reduction can remove.

- **No stable immune-compatible escape: 0/169** (WT ↓ ≥0.5 log AND CD8 ≥70% AND converged).
- The **stable** region (14% of the grid) is *exactly* the two corners where the TIP is
  **inert**: (i) high ν / no reservoir → no evasion, TIP a weak lever (≤0.21 log); and
  (ii) low ν / large reservoir → reservoir saturates CD8 but the TIP can't establish, WT
  ≈ unchanged. Every cell where the TIP *would* suppress WT oscillates (transient, not a
  fixed point).

## The sharp structural statement (any-two-of-three)

An *immune-compatible TIP* needs three things at once:
- **(a)** meaningful immune pressure present,
- **(b)** the TIP establishing/suppressing (which requires ν<1 evasion), and
- **(c)** a stable fixed point to read off.

In this ODE class they are **mutually incompatible** — any two hold, never all three:
`(a)+(b) → oscillation`; `(a)+(c) → TIP inert`; `(b)+(c) → no immunity`. So the escape is
not merely *unobserved* — it is **dynamically excluded from the stable manifold** of these
mean-field equations.

## What this does to the re-audit's open question

The re-audit (correctly) flagged "definitive limit of the ODE program" as overclaimed,
reasoning that "oscillation is routinely fixable." **P1.5 tests that claim and it fails for
the obvious fixes:** the two principled mean-field stabilizations — QSS immunity (P1.3) and
QSS virus (here) — *both* leave the escape regime oscillating. That is strong evidence the
instability is robust.

It is **not proof it's unfixable.** Still untried: (a) Holling-II / handling-time
saturation on the competition; (b) a deliberately Lyapunov-stable reformulation; and most
importantly (c) the genuinely different class — a **stochastic / agent-based** model, where
discreteness and extinction naturally regularize the predator–prey cycle, and where the
Simonetti "1-in-3-infected" defective clones are *individuals*, not a mean field. That is
the right tool for the escape question; another ODE reduction is not.

## Net

The stable build did its job: it converted "the model oscillates, so I can't test the
escape" into a **structural** statement — *immune pressure + TIP-evasion + stability are
incompatible in mean-field TIP ODEs.* Combined with the re-audit's findings (the antagonism
is the single-pool construction; model↔clinic is a loose analogy; the one emergent result
is P1.1's dynamic immune-blunting), the toy-ODE program is now genuinely complete, with the
escape question cleanly handed to a stochastic/agent-based successor.

_Caveats: mean-field ODE, illustrative params; the any-two-of-three statement is empirical
over this grid (and across P1.3–P1.5), not a proven theorem; 2 of several stabilizations tried._
