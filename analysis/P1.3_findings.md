## Goal

The audit gated any quantitative/clinical claim on two things: (1) **stabilize** the
oscillating immune model so magnitudes mean something; (2) test the one **escape
hatch** — can spatial structure (a follicular antigen sanctuary the TIP penetrates
poorly) let systemic WT fall while follicular antigen keeps CD8 primed, breaking the
well-mixed antagonism?

## Part 1 — Stabilized well-mixed (✅ clean, antagonism survives)

Adiabatic elimination: CD8 track antigen with no lag, `E*(A)=Emax·A/(A+K_E)`. First
attempt kept the **exhaustion** term `/(1+A/Kx)` — still oscillated (tail 49×), because
exhaustion (killing *falls* at high antigen) is a **destabilizing positive feedback**.
Removing it (killing monotone-increasing in antigen = stabilizing negative feedback)
gave a **stable** chronic setpoint (tail max/min = 1.000). Note: the audit had already
closed the exhaustion-relief escape hatch, so nothing is lost.

Clean antagonism test (stable endpoints, no time-averaging):
- Under mild stable immunity (kill ≈ 0.57/day), the TIP is a **weak lever**: ≤ **0.17
  log** WT reduction even at ψ=22; below ψ≈16 it does nothing (can't establish).
- Where it acts, WT suppression and CD8 retention trade off **monotonically**: ψ16→22
  gives WT −0.02→−0.17 log while CD8 `E*(A)` falls 0.99→0.86. (The corr = −1.00 here is
  **still by-construction** — only 3 points move, each a monotone transform of the single
  shared antigen scalar — so it is *not* independent evidence of a trade-off; the substance
  is only that suppression and CD8 fall together, as the single-pool structure forces.
  *[Re-audit correction: the earlier "meaningful, not degenerate" framing was rehabilitation
  of a retracted statistic.]*)
- **No immune-compatible TIP** (WT ≥1 log down with CD8 ≥50%): none.
- Stronger stable immunity (kill ≈ 1.9/day) **excludes the TIP entirely** (0 log at
  every ψ).

**=> The antagonism is NOT an oscillation artifact.** With the oscillation removed it
holds cleanly: TIP suppression == antigen removal == CD8 loss, one axis. This is the
audit's "structural" reading, now confirmed in a stable model.

## Part 2 — Spatial escape hatch (⚠️ inconclusive: beyond this tool)

Two compartments — periphery P (TIP works; systemic load = Vw_P) and follicle F (TIP
access φ; φ=0 ⇒ sanctuary) — CD8 systemic, primed by **total** antigen, virus diffusing
P↔F at rate g.

The (φ, g) sweep *appeared* to show escape (systemic WT −1.81 log with CD8 99% kept at
φ=0.5, g=0.03). **It is an artifact.** The convergence check (`p13_check.py`) shows the
2-compartment model is **oscillating / unconverged at every point**: Vw_P swings 10–100×
over time (tail max/min 8–82), never settling. The endpoints — and the "escape" — are
transient snapshots, not fixed points. The script's auto-printed "antagonism BREAKS in
tissue" is therefore **invalid and retracted.**

Why: CD8 killing keyed to *total* (cross-compartment) antigen plus virus diffusion is a
**nonlocal, delayed feedback** that destabilizes even though each compartment alone was
stable. That instability is itself real (spatially-coupled immune control is dynamically
rich) — but it means a simple coupled-ODE with endpoint metrics **cannot resolve** the
escape-hatch question. Patching it would be exactly the "more tuning" the audit warned
against.

**=> The spatial escape hatch is OPEN — neither confirmed nor refuted.** It needs a
purpose-built, stable spatial/stochastic model (germinal-center PDE or agent-based with
explicit local immunity), not another coupled ODE.

## Verdict — the ODE program's honest landing

- **Well-mixed dynamics: TIPs are structurally antagonistic to CD8/vaccinal-effect
  control** — confirmed clean (Part 1), parameter-robust (audit), mechanism clear (TIP
  lives at R0≈1 and can only suppress WT by removing the antigen CD8 needs). *High
  confidence, well-mixed only.*
- **Spatial/tissue: unresolved.** The one mechanism that could break the antagonism
  (antigen sanctuary decoupling) is beyond what a coupled ODE can decide here.
- **All magnitudes illustrative**, not calibrated.

This is where the toy-ODE arc ends cleanly: it answered the well-mixed question
(antagonism real) and correctly identified the boundary of its own resolution (the
spatial question). Going further is a **different modeling project** (P2: a real spatial
model), not another iteration of this one — a good place to bank the result.

## Addendum (2026-06-20) — real-world anchor: Simonetti 5′-leader-defective NSV

Pulled: White/Simonetti **JCI 2023** ([165245](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/165245),
foundational mechanism, OA) + Simonetti et al. **Nat Commun 2026**
([s41467-026-73475-5](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-73475-5); CLAWS assay,
52-person scale-up). It bears directly on the model.

**1. Nature already manufactures the TIP's payload — minus the mobilization.**
Non-suppressible viremia is driven by clonally-expanded T-cell clones (1–3 dominant
clones/person, up to ~10⁷ cells — "1 in 3 infected cells" in patient P1) carrying
5′-leader-defective proviruses (two distinct deletions — 22-nt at HXB2 727–748, 21-nt at 740–760 — hitting DIS / Ψ /
major-splice-donor). Critically, **the genomic RNA is still packaged** (~62–78% of WT NC
binding — d21 62%, d22 78%; *modestly reduced, not a packaging block*) — the
non-infectivity comes from **Env loss** (splice-donor defect → ~5–17× less Env → Env-
deficient virions). So these are **packaged, Env-deficient defective genomes released as
non-infectious virions** — the molecular substrate of a TIP. What they lack is
**trans-complementation / mobilization**: they are single-clone, single-infection events,
so (*my inference, not a JCI claim*) they don't act as classical interfering particles,
which require co-infection to be mobilized. **Our ψ is exactly the missing ingredient**
between natural benign NSV-defectives and a therapeutic TIP.
> _Corrected in multi-agent audit (2026-06-20): "~80%" overstated the better variant
> (true range 62–78%); and the previously-quoted "not interfering particles in the
> classical sense" line was a fetch-model **gloss, NOT in the JCI paper** — de-quoted and
> re-attributed as my own inference. See [AUDIT.md](AUDIT.md)._

**2. The immune-recognition data refines our antagonism — toward SOFTENING it.**
Simonetti (2026): *"intact proviruses that make virus are pruned away, while defective
ones escape the immune system."* JCI mechanism: the persistent defective clones carry
**CTL-escape mutations (58–82 % of epitopes, incl. B*57 Gag TW10/AW10)**, resist
autologous nAbs, and — the load-bearing hypothesis — make **lower levels of immune-
recognition antigens, especially Env.** So immune visibility scales with **WT-antigen
output.** This indicts our well-mixed assumption directly: we killed I_w (WT-only) and
I_d (dual, TIP-producing) at the **same** CD8 rate, but I_d diverts fraction ρ of output
to TIP and makes correspondingly less WT/Env → it should be killed **less.** Differential
killing of the TIP's own factories would **soften the antagonism** — the first
data-grounded reason the well-mixed result might not hold.

**3. It populates the open frontier with real parameters:** a mechanism (antigen-output-
graded immune visibility), clone sizes (~10⁶–10⁷ cells), a compartment (effector-memory,
proliferation-driven), and a cheap plasma assay (**CLAWS**, 5′-leader digital-PCR — the
TIP-relevant locus) to measure the defective fraction.

**Concrete next experiment (data-anchored, beats the generic spatial model):** in the
*stable* P1.3-Part-1 model, make CD8 killing of I_d scale with its WT-antigen output —
`kill(I_d) = (1−ρ)·kill(I_w)` (or an interpolation) instead of equal killing — and re-run
the antagonism test. Does graded immune-visibility open an immune-compatible regime? It's
stable (no oscillation → clean numbers) and grounded in measured biology, so it's a
sharper escape-hatch test than the (unstable) spatial coupling. This is the new P1.4.
